How to Track Your Crypto Portfolio's Real P&L (Average Cost Method)

"How much am I up?" sounds like a simple question until you've bought at three prices, sold a bit and paid fees along the way. Here's how to get a clear answer.

Two kinds of profit

  • Realized P&L: profit or loss you locked in by selling.
  • Unrealized P&L: profit or loss on coins you still hold, at today's price.

Your total result is the two added together. Exchange apps often show only one of them, or only the last trade.

Average cost basis

With the average cost method, every coin you hold carries the same cost: total spent (fees included) ÷ coins bought. When you sell, the coins you sell take that average cost with them; the average of the coins you keep doesn't change.

Worked example

Still holding0.04 BTC, cost $2,517.14average still $62,928.57

Example numbers.

Total result: +$360.14 realized + $362.86 unrealized = +$723.00.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting fees, which makes every coin look cheaper than it was.
  • Recalculating the average after a sell. With average cost, selling doesn't change the average of what you keep.
  • Counting transfers between your own wallets as buys or sells. They're neither.
  • Treating stablecoins as profit. They're cash at $1.
