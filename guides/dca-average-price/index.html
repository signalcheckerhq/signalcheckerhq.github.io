How to Calculate Your Average Price When You DCA Into Crypto

If you buy a coin several times at different prices, what's your real entry price? Most people average the prices. That gives the wrong answer whenever the amounts are different.

The formula

Average price = total money spent ÷ total coins bought

Not the average of the prices. Each buy counts in proportion to how many coins it bought.

Worked example

BuySpentPriceCoins
1$100$1001.0000
2$100$901.1111
3$200$802.5000
Total$4004.6111

Average price = $400 ÷ 4.6111 = $86.75. Averaging the three prices gives $90, which is $3.25 too high because the biggest buy happened at the lowest price.

From the last buy at $80, the price needs to rise about 8.4% (to $86.75) for the whole position to break even, before fees.

Fees change it a little

Add each buy's fee to "money spent" for a more honest average. On small buys with high fees the difference adds up.

Plan the stop before you DCA

DCA plans often put the stop below the last buy level. If every level fills and the stop is hit, you lose on the whole position, not just the first buy. Before you enter, work out: total money spent if all levels fill × distance from your average price to the stop. That's your real risk. If the stop sits above your last DCA level, the last buy can never happen, so the plan doesn't make sense.
