How to Check If a Crypto Signal Group Is Legit (Before You Pay)

Most signal groups post their wins loudly and their losses quietly, or not at all. Before you pay for a "VIP" channel, you can check its record yourself in about an hour. Here's how.

1. Get the full call history, not the highlights

Scroll back at least 30–50 calls, ideally a few months. Screenshots of winners in a pinned message don't count. You want every call: coin, entry, targets, stop, and the time it was posted. If the group deletes or edits old calls, that alone tells you a lot.

2. Check that each call was posted before the move

A "called it!" message after a coin pumps isn't a signal. Note the posting time and compare it with the price chart at that moment. If the entry price was already far below the market when the call went out, nobody could have bought there.

3. Did the entry actually fill?

Limit entries often never fill. The group may still count the trade as a win once price reaches a target. A trade that never filled is not a win; it simply didn't happen. Check whether price really touched the entry after the call was posted.

4. Count the stop-outs, and which came first

For each filled trade, check which happened first: a target or the stop. Many groups report "TP1 hit ✅" and never mention that price then fell through the stop, or that TP2 and TP3 never came. Count a trade as a win only for the targets that were really reached before the stop.

5. Look at profit factor, not just win rate

A 70% win rate sounds great, but if the average win is +2% and the average loss is −6%, the group loses money. Profit factor (total gains ÷ total losses) shows that at a glance: above 1 is profitable, below 1 is not. Our guide Win rate vs profit factor shows the math.

6. Include fees, and DCA levels if there are any

Every trade pays fees on entry and exit. With small targets (1–2%), fees can eat a big share of the profit. If the group uses DCA levels ("add more at X"), calculate the real average price and what happens at the stop when every level filled.

7. Use a big enough sample

Five calls tell you almost nothing. Aim for 30+ filled trades before drawing conclusions, and remember that market conditions change: a good month in a bull run doesn't guarantee the next one.

Example (4 real calls, real prices): one group's last four calls looked like 3 winners in the channel. Checked against real prices: 1 hit all targets (+2.03%), 2 were stopped out after a DCA fill (−3.35% and −5.26%), 1 is still open. That's a 33% win rate, a profit factor of 0.24, and −2.19% on average per closed signal.
