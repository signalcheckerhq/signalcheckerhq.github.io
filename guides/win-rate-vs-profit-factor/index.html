Win Rate vs Profit Factor: Why a 70% Win Rate Can Still Lose Money

Win rate is the most quoted number in trading and the least useful on its own. What matters is how much you make when you win compared with how much you lose when you lose.

Two traders, one surprise

Average loss−6%−2%
Expectancy per trade−0.40%+0.80%

Result of 100 trades of $1,000−$400+$800

Example numbers, before fees.

Trader A wins 7 times out of 10 and still loses money. Trader B loses almost two trades out of three and comes out ahead.

Expectancy: what one trade is worth on average

Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss)

Trader A: 0.70 × 2% − 0.30 × 6% = 1.4% − 1.8% = −0.4% per trade.
Trader B: 0.35 × 6% − 0.65 × 2% = 2.1% − 1.3% = +0.8% per trade.

Profit factor: gains divided by losses

Profit factor = total money from winning trades ÷ total money lost on losing trades

Above 1.0 means the wins outweigh the losses. Trader A: 1.4 ÷ 1.8 = 0.78. Trader B: 2.1 ÷ 1.3 = 1.62. It's one number that already contains both win rate and win size.

R multiples: measure every trade against what you risked

1R is the amount you lose if your stop is hit. A trade that makes twice that is +2R; a stop-out is −1R. Thinking in R makes trades with different sizes comparable and shows quickly whether your targets are worth your stops.

The number most traders never check

How many average wins does it take to cover your biggest loss? If the answer is 5, one bad trade wipes out a week of good ones. Tracking this keeps stops honest.
