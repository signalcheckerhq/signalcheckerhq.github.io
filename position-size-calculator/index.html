How to calculate position size in crypto

Most traders pick a position size by feel: "I'll put $500 in." The problem is that $500 with a tight stop and $500 with a wide stop are completely different risks. Position sizing flips it around. First decide how much you're willing to lose, then let the stop distance tell you how big the position can be.

Risk ($) = account balance × risk %
Loss per coin = |entry − stop| + (entry + stop) × fee %
Position size (coins) = Risk ($) ÷ Loss per coin

Worked example

$5,000 account, 1% risk, BTC long at $108,000 with a stop at $104,500 and a 0.1% fee per side:

If the stop is hit you lose $50 including fees, no matter how much leverage you use.

Does leverage change your risk?

Not if you size by your stop. Leverage only changes how much margin you lock up (here $290.91 at 5x) and where the exchange would liquidate you (about $86,400). The danger is when liquidation sits before your stop: then you can lose more than planned. The calculator warns you when that happens.

What is an R multiple?

1R is what you risk. In the example above, a take profit at $116,000 earns $104.73 after fees, which is +2.09R. Thinking in R makes trades comparable: a strategy that wins 40% of the time can still be profitable if the average win is 2R and the average loss is 1R.

How much should you risk per trade?

Many traders keep it between 0.5% and 2%. The reason is losing streaks. At 1% risk, ten losses in a row leave about 90% of the account. At 5% risk, the same streak leaves about 60%, and you then need +67% just to get back to even. This is math, not advice: pick what fits you.

FAQ

Does this work for shorts?

Yes. Switch to Short: the stop must be above entry and the take profit below it.

Is the liquidation price exact?

No. It's an estimate that ignores maintenance margin and funding. Always check the liquidation price your exchange shows.

Is my data stored?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
